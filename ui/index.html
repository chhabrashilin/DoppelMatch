Speed check: the WebAssembly engine, in your browser

This runs the same C++ matching code, compiled to WebAssembly, on a private book inside your browser. Expect roughly a quarter to a third of the native figure (about 26 million messages/second on one core in the benchmark tables): WebAssembly has no huge pages, no core pinning and a slower memory model. It is the same code, not a re-implementation, so any difference is the platform, not the algorithm.

What was measured, and where

Core i7-1355U laptop under WSL2, GCC 16, one pinned core, 5M-message deterministic workload, best of 15 interleaved runs. The hypervisor takes ~12% of a pinned core, so throughput moves up to 2x between runs and tails above p99 measure the machine. Cache counts are cachegrind simulations, not hardware counters. Details: BENCHMARKS.md.

The rules (all implemented here and in C++)

Price-time priority. A buy matches the lowest-priced sell first; ties go to whoever arrived first. Trades happen at the resting order's price.

Limit orders rest if they can't fully trade. Market orders sweep the book and never rest. IOC trades what it can and cancels the rest. FOK trades everything or nothing. Post-only is rejected if it would take liquidity.

Cancel removes an order. Modify keeps your place in line only if you shrink size at the same price.

Why the C++ version is fast

Prices are array indices, not tree nodes. A bitmap finds the next best price in a couple of instructions. Orders live in preallocated slabs, so nothing allocates while matching. Threads talk through a lock-free ring, and each is pinned to its own core.

Every version of the engine is checked against a deliberately simple reference on millions of random events: outputs must match byte for byte.

Spread capture is cents to a few dollars; inventory PnL is negative in every run.

After a fill the mid moves $4.7 to $10 against the position within 1 s, against an unconditional 1 s standard deviation of $3.49.

A naive "touch means filled" backtest counts 7 times the fills for touch quoting (8,693 vs 1,246).

At 10 bps every strategy loses $55 to $421 in half an hour; even a 0.5 bps rebate leaves all of them negative.

A-S at high risk aversion keeps inventory near zero and bleeds slowest.

ETHUSDT, 25 minutes: the same shape, and no A-S advantage that survives its confidence interval.

What to take from it

  • Passive quoting near the touch loses roughly 1.3 to 2 basis points of the notional it trades, on both assets, whichever strategy is used. The fills it gets are the ones where price is about to move against it.
  • Only a maker rebate above about 1.5 bps, or a real information edge, would change the sign.
  • Avellaneda-Stoikov is an inventory-control tool: it loses less by trading less, it does not earn more per trade.
  • How you model fills changes the answer where retail backtests tend to quote: at the touch.
  • This is about an hour of data on two assets. The limits are part of the result (docs/RESEARCH.md).
